SCRIPT 5: Board of Studies — February Follow-up
================================================
Duration target: ~7 minutes spoken
Speakers: 4 (same as Script 3)
  - KESHAVAN (male, Dean of Faculty, chairing)
  - JAYASHREE (female, CSE chairperson)
  - KUMAR (male, senior academic council member)
  - PRIYA (female, external academic reviewer)

Setup: The February twenty eighth meeting that Script 3 scheduled.
Resolves the deferred Data Mining item, closes the industry
collaboration thread, and reverses one earlier decision. Designed so
the graph shows a decision changing state across meetings.

---

KESHAVAN: Good morning. This is the follow-up to our January meeting.
We deferred one item and approved three. Let's start with the deferred
item. Kumar, you were preparing a justification for retaining Data
Mining.

KUMAR: I was, and I've circulated it. My position has changed somewhat.

PRIYA: Changed how?

KUMAR: I pulled the enrollment data for the last five years, not two.
Eleven and fourteen were the last two years, but before that it was
thirty one, twenty eight, and twenty six. The decline is recent and it
correlates exactly with when we introduced the advanced machine
learning elective.

JAYASHREE: So students are substituting, not losing interest.

KUMAR: That's my reading. Which means removing Data Mining entirely
loses content that nothing else covers. Association rules, frequent
pattern mining, anomaly detection on transactional data.

PRIYA: This is what I suggested in January. Merge rather than remove.

KUMAR: Yes, and I now agree with Priya. I propose we merge Data Mining
with Statistical Learning into a single four credit course. Call it
Data Mining and Statistical Learning.

JAYASHREE: I can work with that. It solves my redundancy concern and
Kumar's coverage concern.

KESHAVAN: Who would teach it?

JAYASHREE: Meera has taught Statistical Learning for three years. She
would be the natural choice, but she's already taking the revised NLP
course and two bridge sessions for the deep learning elective.

KUMAR: I can co-teach. I've taught Data Mining since it was introduced.

KESHAVAN: Then let's do that. Meera and Kumar co-teach Data Mining and
Statistical Learning, four credits, from the next academic year. Kumar,
that reverses our January decision to replace the course. I want the
minutes to show that explicitly.

KUMAR: Agreed.

PRIYA: I support it.

JAYASHREE: In favour.

KESHAVAN: Approved. Item two. Industry collaborations. In January we
prioritised Infosys first, NVIDIA second, Bosch third. Jayashree,
status.

JAYASHREE: Infosys. The memorandum of understanding is drafted. Legal
has reviewed it. It is not signed yet.

KESHAVAN: Why not?

JAYASHREE: One clause. They want first look at student projects for
recruitment. Legal says that as written, it could be read as an
exclusivity arrangement, which would conflict with our placement
policy.

KUMAR: That's a serious conflict. We cannot give one company
exclusivity over student work.

JAYASHREE: Ramesh has gone back to them. They've indicated they'll
accept non exclusive language. We expect the revised draft in ten days.

KESHAVAN: So we are not signing today. Jayashree, present the signature
at the April meeting. Do not let the department sign anything before
legal clears the revised clause.

JAYASHREE: Understood.

KESHAVAN: NVIDIA.

JAYASHREE: Simpler. They've offered a three day GPU computing workshop
for final year and postgraduate students. No cost to us. They provide
instructors and cloud credits. We provide the room and the students.

PRIYA: What's the capacity?

JAYASHREE: Sixty. They've asked for a date in April.

KESHAVAN: Fix it as April twelfth to fourteenth. That's after the
semester examinations begin, so attendance may suffer.

JAYASHREE: Examinations start on the eighteenth. The twelfth to
fourteenth is clear.

KESHAVAN: Then April twelfth. Approved. Bosch.

JAYASHREE: No movement. We deprioritised it in January and I have not
contacted them since.

KUMAR: We should at least respond. Leaving a proposal unanswered for
two months is poor practice.

KESHAVAN: Fair. Jayashree, send a holding response this week. Say we're
interested but cannot commit resources this academic year. Postpone it
formally rather than let it lapse.

JAYASHREE: I'll send it.

KESHAVAN: Item three. The capstone framework. We extended phase one to
fourteen weeks in January. Any consequences?

JAYASHREE: One, raised by Meera. The literature survey requirement was
set for a twelve week phase. With fourteen weeks the requirement is now
too light. We propose adding a comparison matrix. Same minimum of four
papers, but students must compare methods across those papers on common
criteria.

PRIYA: I've seen the draft rubric. Meera sent it last week. It's
reasonable and it's gradeable, which was my concern in January.

KUMAR: How many marks?

JAYASHREE: The fifteen points already allocated to critical analysis
get redistributed. Eight for the matrix, seven for written analysis. No
change to the total.

KESHAVAN: Approved. Priya, are you satisfied the rubric is fair across
graders?

PRIYA: Mostly. I have one reservation. The peer review component gives
five percent for the quality of feedback a team gives to other teams.
Grading the quality of feedback is inherently subjective, and we have
no calibration between graders.

KESHAVAN: What would fix it?

PRIYA: Either drop it to two percent, or have the receiving team rate
the usefulness of the feedback and use that as an input. I'd prefer the
second.

JAYASHREE: Teams rating each other creates its own problems. Friendly
teams rate each other well.

PRIYA: Then anonymise it. Teams don't know who reviewed them.

KUMAR: In a cohort of forty seven students, anonymity is theoretical.

KESHAVAN: Let's not over engineer this. Reduce it to two percent for
this year, collect data, and revisit in a year with actual numbers.
Priya, does that satisfy you?

PRIYA: For one year, yes.

KESHAVAN: Approved at two percent. Item four. Jayashree, you had a note
from the placement panel.

JAYASHREE: Yes. Thirty one of eighty students placed in the TCS drive.
The panel's feedback was that students are strong on theory and weak on
system design. Ramesh has prepared a note proposing a system design
component in the third year curriculum.

KUMAR: That's a real gap. I've heard the same from two other companies.

PRIYA: It's a gap everywhere, not just here.

KESHAVAN: Circulate Ramesh's note before the April meeting. We'll take
it as a full agenda item rather than squeeze it in now. Anything else?

JAYASHREE: Nothing from the department.

KESHAVAN: Then we're done. Next meeting April eighteenth. Jayashree,
you owe us the Infosys signature and the Bosch response. Kumar, the
merged course syllabus. Thank you all.
