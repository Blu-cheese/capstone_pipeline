SCRIPT 4: CSE Department Weekly Meeting — Phase Two Procurement
================================================================
Duration target: ~6 minutes spoken
Speakers: 4 (same as Scripts 1 and 2)
  - JAYASHREE (female, department chairperson)
  - RAMESH (male, senior professor)
  - MEERA (female, NLP professor)
  - PAVAN (male, lab coordinator)

Setup: Department meeting held after the Board of Studies review in
Script 3. Closes out the phase one GPU purchase, opens phase two, and
starts the Infosys collaboration. Continues the budget and deadline
threads from Scripts 1 through 3 so the graph shows evolution.

---

JAYASHREE: Good morning. Three things today. The lab phase two, the
Infosys agreement, and the elective rollout. Pavan, start with the lab.

PAVAN: Ma'am, phase one is complete. Two workstations delivered on the
fourteenth, installed and running. Total spend was two point two lakhs,
exactly the approved figure.

JAYASHREE: Any issues with installation?

PAVAN: One. The lab's existing power circuit could not handle both
machines at full load. We had to add a dedicated line. That cost
eighteen thousand rupees, which I paid from the maintenance head.

RAMESH: Was that approved?

PAVAN: I informed the chairperson before spending. It was an emergency.

JAYASHREE: I approved it verbally. Pavan, put it in writing so the
audit trail is clean. Now, phase two.

PAVAN: Good news here. GPU prices have come down. The same machine that
quoted one point one two lakhs in December is now ninety five thousand.
Two machines come to one point nine lakhs.

MEERA: That's below what we budgeted for phase two.

PAVAN: Yes ma'am. We had planned two point two lakhs again. So we are
thirty thousand under.

JAYASHREE: Then let's use the difference. Meera, you mentioned needing
API credits for the language model module.

MEERA: I did. The new NLP syllabus has students fine-tuning and calling
hosted models. Forty thousand rupees would cover a full semester for
sixty students.

JAYASHREE: We have thirty thousand spare. Can you manage with that?

MEERA: If students share team accounts, yes. I'll restructure the
assignment to be team based rather than individual.

JAYASHREE: Do that. Pavan, raise the phase two purchase order for one
point nine lakhs. What's the delivery timeline?

PAVAN: Vendor says three weeks from purchase order. If I raise it this
week, machines arrive by March fifteenth.

JAYASHREE: March fifteenth is the deadline then. The elective starts in
April, so that gives us buffer. Ramesh, the syllabus.

RAMESH: The Board approved the Applied Deep Learning syllabus with the
revised assessment. Thirty percent mini project, thirty percent
continuous evaluation, forty percent end semester. The mini project is
the production deployment task we discussed.

JAYASHREE: Good. Enrollment?

RAMESH: Forty seven students registered. We capped it at fifty.

MEERA: That's strong for a first offering.

RAMESH: There's one complication. Of those forty seven, nineteen have
not taken Machine Learning. The Board made ML a prerequisite, but that
applies to next year's batch only.

JAYASHREE: So this batch is grandfathered in.

RAMESH: Correct. But nineteen students without ML background in a deep
learning course is a real problem. I'd like to run a bridge module.

JAYASHREE: What would that involve?

RAMESH: Four extra sessions before the semester starts. Linear algebra
refresher, gradient descent, basic classification. Voluntary but
strongly recommended.

MEERA: Who teaches it?

RAMESH: I'll take two sessions. I was hoping Meera takes the other two.

MEERA: I can do that. Schedule them in the last week of March.

JAYASHREE: Approved. Ramesh, you own the bridge module. Now the Infosys
collaboration. The Board prioritised it first among the three proposals.

RAMESH: I spoke to their university relations head last Thursday. They
want a joint research lab focused on applied machine learning. Their
contribution is two engineers on site one day a week, plus compute
credits.

JAYASHREE: And what do they want from us?

RAMESH: Space, and two faculty members committed at least six hours a
week. They also want first look at student projects for hiring.

MEERA: First look at hiring is fine. First rights on intellectual
property would not be.

RAMESH: They have not asked for intellectual property. I raised it
specifically. The draft says jointly owned.

JAYASHREE: Jointly owned is acceptable. Who drafts the memorandum of
understanding from our side?

RAMESH: Legal will draft it. I'll coordinate. They want it signed
before the Board meets on February twenty eighth.

JAYASHREE: That's tight. Ramesh, target March tenth for the draft, and
if it slips we present the status rather than the signature. Do not
rush legal review to hit a date.

RAMESH: Understood.

JAYASHREE: Pavan, one more thing. The capstone peer review is week
twelve this year, not week fourteen. The Board changed phase one to
fourteen weeks. Make sure the lab is bookable that week.

PAVAN: I'll block the lab for the whole of week twelve.

MEERA: On capstone, I have a concern. Phase one is now fourteen weeks,
but the literature survey requirement is still four papers minimum.
Students have more time and the same requirement.

JAYASHREE: What do you propose?

MEERA: Raise it to six papers, or add a comparison matrix. Something
that uses the extra two weeks.

RAMESH: Six papers is a lot for undergraduates.

MEERA: Then the comparison matrix. Same four papers, but they must
compare methods across them on common criteria.

JAYASHREE: I prefer that. Meera, write it into the rubric and send it
to Priya. She's reviewing the rubric for fairness.

MEERA: I'll send it by Friday.

JAYASHREE: Last item. The TCS placement drive results came in. Thirty
one students placed out of eighty who sat. That's a better conversion
than last year's twenty two.

RAMESH: Any feedback from their panel?

JAYASHREE: Yes, and it's useful. They said our students are strong on
theory and weak on system design. Ramesh, that's worth feeding into the
curriculum discussion at the next Board meeting.

RAMESH: I'll prepare a note.

JAYASHREE: Good. Next meeting same time next week. Thank you.
