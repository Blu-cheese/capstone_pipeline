SCRIPT 3: Board of Studies Meeting — Curriculum Review
========================================================
Duration target: ~7 minutes spoken
Speakers: 4
  - KESHAVAN (male, Dean of Faculty, chairing)
  - JAYASHREE (female, CSE chairperson)
  - KUMAR (male, senior academic council member)
  - PRIYA (female, external academic reviewer)

Setup: Board of Studies meeting reviewing curriculum changes. More 
realistic dialogue with interruptions, disagreements, and unresolved 
items. Tests the pipeline's ability to handle messier conversation.

---

KESHAVAN: Alright, let's begin. We have twelve items on the agenda. 
I want to finish in ninety minutes. First item, the curriculum revision 
for the AI and ML program. Jayashree, please present.

JAYASHREE: Thank you sir. So, the department has proposed three major 
changes. First, replacing the Data Mining elective with a new course 
called Applied Deep Learning. Second, updating the NLP course syllabus 
to include large language models. Third, making Machine Learning a 
prerequisite for three advanced electives.

KUMAR: Jayashree, hold on. Why are we removing Data Mining? It's a 
foundational course.

JAYASHREE: Sir, the enrollment has dropped significantly. Last two 
years, only eleven and fourteen students respectively opted for it.

KUMAR: That doesn't mean we remove it. It means we improve it.

PRIYA: Actually, if I may. At our institution, we had the same issue 
with Data Mining. We merged it with the statistical learning course. 
That approach worked well.

JAYASHREE: We considered that. But we already have an advanced ML 
course covering similar ground. Adding another would be redundant.

KUMAR: I'm not convinced. Data Mining covers things like association 
rules, frequent pattern mining. Things not in standard ML courses.

KESHAVAN: Let's table this specific point. Kumar, can you prepare a 
brief justification for keeping Data Mining by next meeting?

KUMAR: Yes, I'll have it ready.

KESHAVAN: Jayashree, continue with the other changes.

JAYASHREE: The NLP update. We want to include a module on transformer 
architectures, fine-tuning, and practical deployment of language models. 
The current syllabus stops at word embeddings and basic sequence models.

PRIYA: That's a reasonable update. Industry is moving fast here. 
What's the assessment like?

JAYASHREE: Sixty percent continuous evaluation, forty percent end 
semester. We have a mandatory group project where students build and 
evaluate an NLP application.

PRIYA: Good. What about the computational requirements? Training 
transformers needs significant resources.

JAYASHREE: We're using pre-trained models and fine-tuning. Not training 
from scratch. The lab has enough GPUs for fine-tuning experiments.

KUMAR: How many GPUs exactly?

JAYASHREE: Currently four. We're procuring four more.

KUMAR: With what budget?

JAYASHREE: Two point two lakhs approved for phase one. Phase two 
will be requested next quarter.

KESHAVAN: Moving on. The third change, prerequisites.

JAYASHREE: We want Machine Learning to be a mandatory prerequisite 
for Deep Learning, Computer Vision, and Reinforcement Learning. 
Currently these are open to any student.

PRIYA: I strongly support this. We've seen too many students struggling 
in advanced electives because they lack ML fundamentals.

KUMAR: Agreed on Deep Learning. But Computer Vision? That's more 
linear algebra and image processing. ML prerequisite might be overkill.

JAYASHREE: The modern computer vision curriculum is basically deep 
learning applied to images. CNNs, vision transformers, generative 
models. Without ML background, students can't follow.

KUMAR: Fine, I'll concede on that.

PRIYA: One question on implementation. What happens to students 
currently enrolled without the prerequisite?

JAYASHREE: Grandfathered in. The prerequisite applies to batches 
starting next academic year.

KESHAVAN: Alright, voting on the package. Three changes. First, 
Data Mining replacement, deferred pending Kumar's review. Second, 
NLP syllabus update. Third, prerequisite changes for advanced electives. 
All in favor of items two and three?

KUMAR: In favor.

PRIYA: In favor.

JAYASHREE: In favor.

KESHAVAN: Approved. Jayashree, please update the curriculum document 
and circulate by Friday. Kumar, Data Mining decision goes to next 
meeting. Moving to item two. The capstone project framework.

JAYASHREE: We've revised the capstone timeline. Phase one is now 
fourteen weeks instead of twelve. Based on student feedback about 
workload. Assessment split remains thirty seventy between phase one 
and phase two.

KUMAR: Fourteen weeks for phase one? That's more than the phase two 
timeline of sixteen weeks. Doesn't seem balanced.

JAYASHREE: Phase two is primarily implementation. Phase one is 
literature review, problem formulation, and prototype design. It 
genuinely takes longer when done properly.

PRIYA: What's the literature survey requirement?

JAYASHREE: Minimum four papers. Students must critically analyze, 
not just summarize.

KUMAR: How do you grade critical analysis? That's subjective.

JAYASHREE: We have a rubric. Ten points for summary. Fifteen points 
for critical analysis, which includes identifying gaps, limitations, 
comparison across papers. Specific criteria for each.

PRIYA: Send me the rubric. I can review it for fairness across 
teams and graders.

JAYASHREE: I'll email it this evening.

KESHAVAN: The extension to fourteen weeks, I approve. What about the 
mandatory peer review Chetana mentioned in her note?

JAYASHREE: Yes. Each team presents their Phase 1 to two other teams. 
Teams give structured feedback using a rubric. This happens in week 
twelve, two weeks before submission.

KUMAR: Is this graded?

JAYASHREE: Yes. Five percent of the phase one grade is based on 
the quality of feedback given to other teams, not just received.

PRIYA: I like this. It incentivizes engagement with peers' work.

KESHAVAN: Approved. Anything else on capstone?

JAYASHREE: Not for now.

KESHAVAN: Item three. External industry collaborations. We received 
three proposals. One from Infosys for a joint research lab. One 
from Bosch for an embedded systems elective. One from NVIDIA for 
GPU computing workshops.

KUMAR: Infosys is the most committed. They've done good work with 
other institutions.

PRIYA: I know the Bosch proposal well. It's genuinely good for 
students interested in automotive software. Hands-on with real 
embedded hardware.

JAYASHREE: NVIDIA workshops would complement our AI program well.

KESHAVAN: We're running low on time. Let me propose we approve 
exploring all three, but prioritize based on resources. Infosys 
first given scope. Then NVIDIA workshops since they're shorter 
duration. Bosch third since embedded isn't our primary focus area.

KUMAR: Fine with me.

PRIYA: Agreed.

JAYASHREE: Agreed.

KESHAVAN: Okay, that's approved. We'll cover items four through 
twelve next meeting. Scheduled for February twenty eighth. Thank 
you all.
